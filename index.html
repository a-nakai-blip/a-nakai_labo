ABOUT THE LAB

About Nakai Laboratory

Connecting scientific analysis with real-world practice.

Nakai Laboratory studies human movement, sport, and recreational activity. We use sport biomechanics, sports informatics, video-based measurement, and quantitative analysis to understand movement and performance. Our goal is to translate research findings into practical knowledge for coaching, physical education, health promotion, outdoor education, and inclusive recreation.

CURRENT RESEARCH

Ongoing Research Projects

Current projects supported by competitive research funding and collaborative research activities.

Our current research examines how movement patterns emerge, how people maintain participation in physical and recreational activities, and how practical environments can support safe and effective movement. One project investigates whether high crawling in young children functions as a transitional movement pattern associated with anticipatory postural control. A second project examines the kinematic mechanisms through which young children acquire change-of-direction movements. A third project tests whether environmental arrangements used by childcare professionals can be generalized to encourage children to extend their hands when falling. Related work explores the continuity of university outdoor leaders’ activities, the determinants of change-of-direction running performance, and accessible video-based methods for analyzing sport skills. Across these studies, we combine observational, biomechanical, and field-based approaches to develop evidence that can inform education, coaching, childcare, and recreation practice.
